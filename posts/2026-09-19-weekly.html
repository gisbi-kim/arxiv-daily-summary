주간 동향

September 14 to 18 produced a dense robot-evidence week: 1121 deduplicated pastweek papers and 515 ROI papers, with Robot Learning at 162 and 3D/Scene at 67. The count spike matters less than the common research decision. VLA papers, SLAM papers, tactile manipulation papers, embodied navigation papers, world-model papers, and deployment-efficiency papers all ask whether a particular piece of evidence is valid enough to change action.

APRL can turn that into a shared benchmark family where maps, tactile state, action tokens, navigation beliefs, world-model rollouts, and compressed multimodal context must predict the same downstream transition failures.

Breaking the Vision-Action Shortcut: Latent Interface Training for Generalizable Robotics Foundation Models

Reading depth: Tier A - abstract-only

Status quo belief: Robot foundation models can learn action correlations from pretrained visual features and look strong in distribution.

Friction: The abstract says task-irrelevant visual cues can correlate with demonstrated actions and then break under visual distribution shift.

Conceptual move: Train an image-free spatial-goal action prior first, then constrain visual conditioning through a pose-supervised latent interface.

Mechanism: LIT conditions action chunks on language, robot state, and terminal SE(3) pose, then makes the latent interface reconstruct that pose as the only visual pathway.

Decisive evidence: [Abstract]: The paper evaluates LIT across Pi0.5, MolmoAct2, FAST-WAM, and ImageWAM.; [Abstract]: It reports 3.87-10.70 percentage-point LIBERO-Plus gains and 13.30-16.70 point real-world gains under unseen camera, lighting, and distractor shifts.; [Inference]: APRL should test whether a latent interface prevents shortcuts by attacking camera, lighting, and distractor correlations separately.

Falsification frontier: If the interface still tracks nuisance visual changes more than terminal-pose-relevant state, shortcut mitigation is incomplete.

Adversarial read: Hold action priors fixed and perturb camera pose, background, distractor objects, and language goals independently.

Transferable thinking tool: Do not let the visual encoder talk to the action head until the interface names the spatial variable it carries.

ShieldVLA: Feasibility-Aware Safety Alignment for Vision-Language-Action Models

Reading depth: Tier A - abstract-only

Status quo belief: Safety fine-tuning for VLAs often trades reward against soft expected-cost penalties.

Friction: The abstract says such methods can leave residual constraint violations or become overly conservative, especially without dense safety labels.

Conceptual move: Approximate a Hamilton-Jacobi reachability value from visual observations and use it to gate policy optimization.

Mechanism: ShieldVLA separates reward maximization in feasible regions from recovery near unsafe states and uses rubric-based VLM safety scores as critic targets.

Decisive evidence: [Abstract]: Across five benchmarks, ShieldVLA reports 57 percent average cumulative safety-cost reduction.; [Abstract]: It reports +0.13 task-success improvement over SafeVLA.; [Inference]: APRL should report when the safety critic changes the action, not only final safety cost.

Falsification frontier: If rubric-derived safety targets miss physical near-collisions, the critic may appear safe while failing geometry-level constraints.

Adversarial read: Stress states near the feasibility boundary with distractors, partial observability, and recovery choices.

Transferable thinking tool: Safety alignment should be a feasibility gate with recovery semantics, not just a scalar cost penalty.

What Makes an Efficient VLA? Navigating Action-Head Design, Scaling, and Latency

Reading depth: Tier A - abstract-only

Status quo belief: Efficient VLA design can be framed as choosing smaller modules or faster inference tricks.

Friction: The abstract says action-head initialization is the largest lever and helps at every module scale without adding latency.

Conceptual move: Measure action-head design, module scale, and on-device latency under a fixed training pipeline instead of comparing heterogeneous VLA recipes.

Mechanism: Copying the last transformer layers into the action head keeps attention on instruction object nouns and close to the backbone weight space.

Decisive evidence: [Abstract]: Flow matching, heavier decoders, and extra inference passes reverse or lose value once the head is aligned.; [Abstract]: Further scaling has diminishing in-domain returns near the size used by current pi-series VLAs.; [Inference]: APRL should pair VLA accuracy with measured latency and action-head alignment diagnostics.

Falsification frontier: If copied-layer alignment fails on contact-rich or cross-embodiment tasks, the recipe may be benchmark-specific.

Adversarial read: Latency-pair aligned and misaligned heads under object-noun swaps, unseen tools, and real robot timing pressure.

Transferable thinking tool: Measure whether capacity buys action alignment before spending latency budget.

Modality-Autoregressive World-Action Models

Reading depth: Tier A - abstract-only

Status quo belief: WAMs are often treated as future RGB predictors with an action head attached.

Friction: RGB prediction may be expensive and not consistently useful when depth, point tracks, and visual features carry more action-relevant state.

Conceptual move: Autoregressively denoise future modalities before predicting actions so each modality can condition on earlier generated evidence.

Mechanism: The abstract says ModAR predicts point tracks, DINO features, and depth maps and then predicts actions from the modality sequence.

Decisive evidence: [Abstract]: The paper reports that point tracks, DINO features, and depth maps help, while future RGB does not provide a consistent benefit.; [Abstract]: ModAR reports 75 percent average success versus 72 percent for a video-model-initialized WAM while using about 20x fewer training FLOPs.; [Inference]: APRL should score each predicted modality by action delta and recovery value before paying RGB-generation cost.

Falsification frontier: If RGB prediction becomes the strongest signal under contact-rich or human-demonstration transfer tasks, the modality ranking is task-specific.

Adversarial read: Averaged success can hide failure families; test modality order under occlusion, geometry corruption, and distractor motion.

Transferable thinking tool: Ask which future modality earns the right to condition the next action.

PanoGS-SLAM: Panoramic 3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM

Reading depth: Tier A - abstract-only

Status quo belief: Dense 3DGS-SLAM often assumes narrow-FoV pinhole cameras and relies on photometric optimization.

Friction: Limited angular coverage weakens pose observability and destabilizes optimization under rapid motion and large viewpoint changes.

Conceptual move: Render and optimize directly in the spherical domain with panoramic photometric constraints and depth-guided Gaussian initialization.

Mechanism: The abstract names sphere-consistent photometric loss and depth-guided Gaussian initialization as the stabilization path.

Decisive evidence: [Abstract]: The paper says controlled field-of-view experiments show monotonic improvement as angular coverage increases.; [Abstract]: It reports outperforming geometric and GS-based baselines in tracking accuracy and rendering quality on panoramic benchmarks.; [Inference]: APRL should treat camera FoV as a map-trust variable, not only an acquisition detail.

Falsification frontier: If panoramic benefit disappears in dynamic clutter or robot-mounted motion blur, FoV alone is not the governing factor.

Adversarial read: Rendering quality and pose accuracy can move together without improving downstream navigation; task-level recovery tests are needed.

Transferable thinking tool: Map representation claims should expose the sensor geometry that made optimization trustworthy.

ActionPiece: Rethinking Action Tokenization for Autoregressive Vision-Language-Action Models

Reading depth: Tier A - abstract-only

Status quo belief: Action tokenizers are often judged by pointwise reconstruction error such as MSE.

Friction: Small pointwise errors can still distort local action adjustments that matter for context-dependent control.

Conceptual move: Evaluate and train tokenization with physical rank consistency, supervising near-far order in representation and codeword assignment.

Mechanism: The abstract says ActionPiece augments reconstruction with representation and quantization objectives that preserve physical action relationships.

Decisive evidence: [Abstract]: The paper argues MSE misses diminished, distorted, or reversed context-specific action adjustments.; [Abstract]: It reports 94.8 percent on LIBERO and 68.8 percent on unseen LIBERO-Plus under the same Qwen3-VL-4B setup.; [Inference]: APRL should rank action tokenizers by whether recovered actions preserve local intervention order, not only by average error.

Falsification frontier: If PRC improves while closed-loop recovery and contact-sensitive success do not, the preserved rank is not the relevant physical relation.

Adversarial read: Rank consistency may reward smooth action neighborhoods that fail under discontinuous contact events.

Transferable thinking tool: Ask what physical relation the discrete action vocabulary preserves.

AMB3R-SLAM: Kilometer-scale SLAM with Hierarchical Backend

Reading depth: Tier A - abstract-only

Status quo belief: Dense visual SLAM often depends on local optimization and static-world assumptions that become fragile in long dynamic traversals.

Friction: Kilometer-scale trajectories over more than 10k frames need online tracking, global consistency, and dynamic-scene tolerance on practical hardware.

Conceptual move: Couple a low-latency front-end with a hierarchical backend that progressively enforces consistency and can accept stereo, RGB-D, or LiDAR as extra evidence.

Mechanism: The abstract says the system avoids bundle adjustment tied to a static-world assumption and reduces ATE on VBR and Oxford Spires by over 70 percent.

Decisive evidence: [Abstract]: The method handles kilometer-scale trajectories over 10k frames on a single consumer GPU.; [Abstract]: Additional LiDAR input reduces ATE to sub-meter level on KITTI and VBR datasets.; [Inference]: APRL should evaluate SLAM backends by when they accept, postpone, or reject map updates under dynamic structure.

Falsification frontier: If dynamic-scene robustness comes mainly from benchmark composition rather than map-update logic, the hierarchy may not transfer to robot deployment.

Adversarial read: Stress with repeated corridors, dynamic occluders, illumination shifts, and online relocalization after tracking loss.

Transferable thinking tool: Treat SLAM as an evidence hierarchy that grants map authority at different spatial scales.

DexTouch-WM: Learning Action-Conditioned Tactile World Models from Human Touch for Dexterous Robot Manipulation
