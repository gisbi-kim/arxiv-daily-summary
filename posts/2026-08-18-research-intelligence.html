#1Tier A - abstract-only

Imagining Recovery: Inference-Time Counterfactual Realignment for Vision-Language-Action Models

VLA recovery - counterfactual rollout - training-free realignment

Status quo belief

A deployed VLA is usually expected to continue from the current observation or require failure data, retraining, or an external recovery agent.

Friction / anomaly

Mid-episode goal changes, scene perturbations, and robot-state deviations can break a frozen policy even when a recent viable state still exists.

Hidden premise

A synthetic continuation from a viable state can supply enough evidence to realign the physical robot without unsafe trial-and-error.

Conceptual move

Treat recovery as counterfactual realignment: imagine the nominal continuation, minimally move robot and scene back onto it, then return control to the frozen policy.

Evidence trace

  • [Abstract]CoRe is a training-free framework that recovers a frozen VLA at inference time without failure data.
  • [Abstract]The authors report improved success and reduced physical restorations across simulators, VLA backbones, and real-world settings.
  • [Inference]APRL should measure whether imagined recovery changes the next action before more physical correction is attempted.

Mechanism

The abstract describes detecting deviation, synthesizing observations from a recent viable state, and realigning the robot and scene before policy handoff.

Falsification frontier

If counterfactual continuations only help small geometric perturbations but fail under contact-rich state uncertainty, the recovery authority is narrower than the headline suggests.

Adversarial read

Stress false deviation detection, ambiguous viable states, and cases where the imagined observation is plausible but contact state is wrong.

Transferable thinking tool

Recover by testing the action path that should have happened, not by immediately retraining or handing control away.

Strong for frozen VLA deployment with recoverable state deviations; weaker where the viable state is not observable or safe to reconstruct.

#2Tier A - abstract-only

ViTaR: Visuo-Tactile Residual Adaptation for Foundation VLA Manipulation

contact-rich manipulation - tactile residuals - bounded adaptation

Status quo belief

Foundation VLAs encode broad visual-semantic priors but often treat contact as hidden state or let tactile feedback directly rewrite action generation.

Friction / anomaly

The same visual state can require different actions when contact is established, lost, or destabilized.

Hidden premise

Tactile feedback should have bounded residual authority rather than unbounded control over the policy.

Conceptual move

Reframe tactile sensing from a primary action-generating input into an execution-layer residual that corrects a VLA when contact evidence demands it.

Evidence trace

  • [Abstract]The paper names contact-rich deployment as a blind spot for foundation VLA manipulation.
  • [Abstract]It argues against both internal VLA modification and unbounded tactile influence over actions.
  • [Inference]APRL should separate visual-semantic intent from contact residual authority in manipulation evaluation.

Mechanism

The abstract positions tactile residual adaptation as a way to keep VLA priors while adding local contact sensitivity.

Falsification frontier

If tactile residuals improve success only by overriding the VLA on most steps, the method is not a bounded adaptation layer.

Adversarial read

Use contact loss, false tactile events, slippery objects, and visually identical contact states to test whether residual authority is calibrated.

Transferable thinking tool

Give contact a veto or correction channel, not a blank check to replace the policy.

Strong for tactile manipulation; less direct for tasks where critical state is visual or proprioceptive rather than contact-based.

#3Tier A - abstract-only

Robo-Dopamine 2.0: History-Conditioned and OOD-Aware Process Reward Modeling for Robotic Manipulation

process reward - history conditioning - OOD manipulation failure

Status quo belief

Robot RL refinement often relies on sparse terminal success or static before-after visual rewards.

Friction / anomaly

Static rewards cannot distinguish robust variations from task-invalid off-trajectory behavior under scene changes and compounding errors.

Hidden premise

A reward model needs execution history and an OOD-sensitive comparison interface to score progress rather than appearance.

Conceptual move

Turn visual reward modeling into a process assessment problem conditioned on trajectory history and distribution shift.

Evidence trace

  • [Abstract]The paper states that learned visual reward models relying on static before-after observations suffer temporal ambiguity.
  • [Abstract]It proposes history-conditioned and OOD-aware process reward modeling for robotic manipulation.
  • [Inference]APRL should evaluate whether a reward curve predicts recoverable deviation before terminal failure.

Mechanism

The abstract introduces a history- and OOD-aware process reward model with a pairwise prediction interface.

Falsification frontier

If history conditioning correlates mainly with video length or scene identity, it may reward dataset artifacts instead of progress.

Adversarial read

Compare trajectories with the same start and end frames but different contact mistakes, recovery paths, and object-state histories.

Transferable thinking tool

Use the shape of a trajectory as reward evidence, not just its first and last frames.

Strong for long-horizon manipulation refinement; weaker for simple tasks with unambiguous terminal labels.

#4Tier A - abstract-only

GaussMemory: Task-Driven 3D Gaussian Scene Memory for Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation

3D Gaussian memory - task-driven update - long-horizon manipulation

Status quo belief

3D robot memories are often passive recorders that update visible elements through fixed rules.

Friction / anomaly

A background wall and a grasp target should not receive the same update budget during long-horizon manipulation.

Hidden premise

A robot can learn what to remember, update, or discard from task success rather than from hand-designed map heuristics.

Conceptual move

Make 3D Gaussian scene memory task-driven: memory management becomes part of the manipulation policy's evidence contract.

Evidence trace

  • [Abstract]The paper says existing 3D memory systems treat critical objects and irrelevant background with equal importance.
  • [Abstract]It proposes active, task-driven spatial memory for long-horizon robotic manipulation.
  • [Inference]APRL should score map memory by whether it preserves the object state that changes the next manipulation step.

Mechanism

The abstract argues for end-to-end learning of object precision, update aggressiveness, and discard behavior.

Falsification frontier

If learned memory improves rendering but not action recovery after occlusion or rearrangement, it is not task-driven enough.

Adversarial read

Use distractor objects, occluded targets, and task switches where remembering the wrong object actively hurts the policy.

Transferable thinking tool

Treat memory update as a control decision with a downstream task metric.

Strong for object-centric manipulation; weaker for pure localization where every geometric feature may carry tracking value.

#5Tier A - abstract-only

MotionGS-SLAM: Event-Modulated Gaussian Splatting for Motion-Blur Robust SLAM

3DGS SLAM - event camera - motion blur robustness

Status quo belief

Vision SLAM often treats motion blur as degraded input that should be restored before mapping.

Friction / anomaly

Deblurring is ill-posed, and catastrophic tracking failure can occur when fast motion destroys frame-based evidence.

Hidden premise

Event timing can constrain blur formation directly inside the Gaussian rendering and mapping model.

Conceptual move

Move from removing blur artifacts to forward-modeling blur with event-modulated Gaussian rasterization.

Evidence trace

  • [Abstract]The paper says current vision SLAM can fail catastrophically under motion blur.
  • [Abstract]It reformulates blur handling as a constrained forward problem inside the rendering pipeline.
  • [Inference]APRL should compare event-constrained maps against frame-only maps under controlled angular velocity and lighting.

Mechanism

The abstract describes an event-modulated Gaussian kernel that adapts rasterization using microsecond event evidence.

Falsification frontier

If gains vanish when blur is paired with low texture or dynamic objects, event modulation may only solve one degradation channel.

Adversarial read

Stress fast camera rotation, low light, sparse event rates, and moving objects separately.

Transferable thinking tool

When perception is degraded, model the sensor failure mode rather than pretending the clean observation exists.

Strong for event-camera SLAM; weaker for robots without asynchronous sensing.

  • [Abstract]It reports that current models struggle with this setting.
  • [Inference]APRL should report which camera changed the decision in a multi-view robot or driving task.

Evidence of Absence: Cross-Modal Abductive Risk Perception to Sustain World Models When Vision Fails

world model risk - missing visual evidence - cross-modal abduction

Status quo belief

World-state models assume visual observations populate entities, relations, context, and predictive cues.

Friction / anomaly

Occlusion or visual degradation can remove exactly the evidence a robot needs to preserve risk predictions.

Hidden premise

The absence of expected co-evidence from one modality can itself indicate a hidden cause when a complementary modality remains informative.

Conceptual move

Sustain the world model through abductive cross-modal risk perception, instantiated with acoustic cues when vision fails.

Evidence trace

  • [Abstract]The paper asks how to sustain a structured world-state model when visual observations are missing.
  • [Abstract]It treats absence of expected co-evidence as evidence of a hidden cause and instantiates the idea acoustically.
  • [Inference]APRL should evaluate whether missing visual evidence changes the stop, search, or avoidance action.

Mechanism

The abstract describes microphone-array bearing and approach-rate evidence used when the primary visual modality is occluded or degraded.

Falsification frontier

Adversarial read

Transferable thinking tool

Strong for degraded perception and mobile robots; weaker for tasks where complementary modalities are unavailable.
