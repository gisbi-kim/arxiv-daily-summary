#1Tier A - abstract-only

IndustrialVLA-Bench: A Traceable Multi-Axis Evaluation of Open Robot Policy Models

VLA/WAM evaluation - evidence tiers - robustness and deployment cost

Status quo belief

Released VLA and WAM systems are often compared across incompatible protocols and single aggregate task scores.

Friction / anomaly

Clean LIBERO averages can be close while robustness, paraphrase sensitivity, latency, memory, and evidence status differ sharply.

Hidden premise

Robot-policy comparisons should be valid only when checkpoint, inference configuration, random seeds, and evidence tier are traceable.

Conceptual move

Evaluate released VLA and WAM systems under one reporting schema with separate clean capability, non-language robustness, instruction sensitivity, and execution-cost axes.

Evidence trace

  • [Abstract]Clean LIBERO averages differ by only 1.58 points while robustness and paraphrase summaries span 14.62 and 31.08 points.
  • [Abstract]Strict comparisons are restricted to protocol-faithful systems, with weaker evidence tiers kept visible.
  • [Inference]APRL should publish robot-policy leaderboards with protocol tier, cost, robustness, and instruction-sensitivity gates before ranking models.

Mechanism

The abstract reports six released systems, three complete seed evaluations, protocol-faithful tiers, and explicit latency, memory, runtime mode, and evidence status.

Falsification frontier

If protocol-faithful systems keep the same ranking across clean, robustness, paraphrase, and deployment-cost axes, the multi-axis split is less decision-changing.

Adversarial read

The benchmark still depends on the chosen task families; contact-rich or navigation tasks may expose different VLA/WAM trade-offs.

Transferable thinking tool

Do not compare released robot policies until the evidence tier and deployment cost are first-class columns.

Strong for released manipulation policies; needs new axes for mobile robots, surgical robots, and field navigation.

#2Tier A - abstract-only

VLAQuantBench: Closed-Loop Evaluation of Post-Training Quantization for Vision-Language-Action Models

VLA quantization - precision assignment - closed-loop robustness

Status quo belief

Post-training quantization is often judged by memory savings and static numerical error.

Friction / anomaly

Small precision choices can make a VLA fail in closed loop even when offline replay or universal sensitivity heuristics look acceptable.

Hidden premise

Quantization policy is part of the controller, so layer scope, format, calibration, and protected projections must be evaluated through rollout success.

Conceptual move

Treat VLA quantization as a controlled closed-loop benchmark with recipe-dependent precision assignments instead of universal layer-sensitivity rules.

Evidence trace

  • [Abstract]Under uncalibrated W4A4 round-to-nearest quantization, expanding a pi0.5 action-head subset from 126 to 167 layers raises success from 7.0% to 70.5%.
  • [Abstract]For OpenVLA-OFT, protecting one 28,672-parameter output projection restores near-baseline success.
  • [Inference]APRL should gate compressed robot policies by rollout families and action-head/projection protection, not only model-size savings.

Mechanism

The abstract reports 409 runs and 94,574 simulation episodes across four models, LIBERO, and additional simulation benchmark families.

Falsification frontier

If physical robot runs do not mirror simulation-sensitive precision assignments, the benchmark needs hardware-specific kernels and calibration axes.

Adversarial read

A recipe that rescues one policy can damage another; the abstraction should force model-specific assignment rather than a single compression rule.

Transferable thinking tool

Compression is acceptable only after naming which action-critical projection or layer scope is protected.

Direct for VLA deployment; less direct for modular policies where quantized perception is separated from control.

#3Tier A - abstract-only

Beyond Reconstruction Error: Analytical and Data-Driven Action Tokenization for Autoregressive Vision-Language-Action Models

action tokenization - closed-loop criterion - decoder stability

Status quo belief

Discrete action representations are commonly selected by reconstruction fidelity or rate-distortion alone.

Friction / anomaly

A tokenization that reconstructs actions well can be harder for a sequence model to predict and can reduce closed-loop control success.

Hidden premise

Action tokens should be judged by geometric fidelity, sequence predictability, decoder stability, and rollout behavior together.

Conceptual move

Compare analytical, linear data-driven, and nonlinear tokenizations under one interface and test how rankings change across diagnostics and LIBERO rollouts.

Evidence trace

  • [Abstract]PCA has lower nominal reconstruction error than Temporal-DCT but lower mean seen-task success across three policy-training seeds.
  • [Abstract]An autoencoder reduces reconstruction error further yet does not yield the strongest policy and is more sensitive to discrete token perturbations.
  • [Inference]APRL should choose robot action tokens using rollout and perturbation diagnostics, not reconstruction error alone.

Mechanism

The abstract contrasts PCA, Temporal-DCT, and autoencoder-style representations over rate-distortion, sequence modeling, token perturbation, and 3,500 rollouts.

Falsification frontier

If token ranking becomes stable under larger model capacity and more data, the reported reversals may reflect underfitting rather than token semantics.

Adversarial read

Seed-level reversals imply evaluation must report uncertainty; otherwise a tokenization can look superior by chance.

Transferable thinking tool

Treat action representation as a controllability contract, not a compression artifact.

Strong for autoregressive VLA control; needs adaptation for continuous diffusion policies.

#4Tier A - abstract-only

RoboTwin-Phys: Do WAMs and VLAs Understand the Physical World?

physical-condition diversity - WAM/VLA robustness - manipulation benchmark

Status quo belief

Manipulation benchmarks vary appearance, layout, and visual observations while often keeping physical parameters fixed.

Friction / anomaly

Policies that survive visual randomization can degrade when mass, friction, or joint dynamics change.

Hidden premise

A robot policy has not learned a physical task unless it remains stable across plausible physical-condition changes.

Conceptual move

Make physical-condition diversity an explicit benchmark dimension with continuous variation of 13 physical attributes.

Evidence trace

  • [Abstract]The benchmark varies mass, friction, joint dynamics, and other physical attributes within physically plausible ranges.
  • [Abstract]Representative WAMs and VLAs can degrade markedly under physical-condition changes despite visual/layout randomization.
  • [Inference]APRL should add physical-parameter sweeps to manipulation evaluation before claiming real-world robustness.

Mechanism

The abstract releases more than 5,000 expert demonstrations with ground-truth physical parameters and evaluates representative WAMs and VLAs under those changes.

Falsification frontier

If physical-parameter estimates are unavailable or unidentifiable from robot observations, policy conditioning may not transfer beyond simulation.

Adversarial read

Mass and friction sweeps should be combined with sensor noise and contact geometry shifts; isolated physics axes can understate coupled failures.

Transferable thinking tool

Ask whether a policy understands the physical variable that changes the outcome, not just the pixels.

Strong for simulation-to-real manipulation; weaker for tasks dominated by semantic planning rather than contact physics.

#5Tier A - abstract-only

Dual Covariance Gaussian Splatting SLAM: Decoupling Rendering and Registration for Robust Real-Time Tracking

3DGS SLAM - rendering versus tracking covariance - real-time registration

Status quo belief

3DGS SLAM often uses one Gaussian covariance for both rendering quality and frame-to-map registration.

Friction / anomaly

Photometric optimization flattens covariances against surfaces, while robust registration needs uncertainty that reflects sensor geometry.

Hidden premise

A map primitive can serve rendering and tracking only if the two uncertainty contracts are separated.

Conceptual move

Keep one Gaussian mean but maintain separate rendering and tracking covariances, with tracking covariance derived from an RGB-D sensor noise model.

Evidence trace

  • [Abstract]The paper identifies conflicting demands between rendering covariance and registration covariance.
  • [Abstract]It reports robust tracking across TUM RGB-D, ScanNet, Replica, and two RealSense D435i outdoor sequences at about 60 FPS.
  • [Inference]APRL should evaluate 3DGS maps by whether their uncertainty supports tracking decisions, not only rendering quality.

Mechanism

The abstract also uses tracking covariances as Gaussian anchors for image corners to add constraints where depth geometry is weak.

Falsification frontier

If separate covariances do not improve downstream navigation or loop-closure recovery, the gain may remain a tracking-level improvement.

Adversarial read

Dynamic scenes, rolling shutter, and reflective surfaces can break the assumed sensor-noise covariance contract.

Transferable thinking tool

Split visual fidelity and pose-estimation uncertainty into separate map responsibilities.

Direct for RGB-D 3DGS SLAM; needs separate treatment for LiDAR-only and monocular pipelines.

#6Tier A - abstract-only

ArborSplat: Online Semantic Gaussian Splatting SLAM for Orchards

semantic 3DGS SLAM - orchard structures - online map semantics

Status quo belief

Appearance-driven 3DGS SLAM can preserve photometric detail while losing thin, task-critical structures.

Friction / anomaly

Orchard robots need trunks, trellises, and fruit in the map, but image-to-3D semantic transfer is unreliable for thin structures.

Hidden premise

Semantic capacity should be allocated according to robot-relevant structure, not only image reconstruction error.

Conceptual move

Optimize semantics directly on the Gaussian map using LiDAR odometry, class-specific height bands, ground-plane constraints, and online multi-view evidence fusion.

Evidence trace

  • [Abstract]The method targets apple and pear orchards across dormancy, flowering, and harvesting.
  • [Abstract]It keeps ATE below 0.5 m on all 12 traversals and improves mIoU while running faster than compared semantic GS-SLAM systems.
  • [Inference]APRL should score map semantics on structures that change robot behavior, especially thin or underrepresented classes.

Mechanism

The abstract rejects labels inconsistent with local ground surface or monocular depth and reserves Gaussian capacity for underrepresented structures.

Falsification frontier

If class-specific height bands do not transfer to other crop geometries or indoor clutter, the semantic prior may be domain-bound.

Adversarial read

A ground-plane prior can reject valid labels on sloped or occluded terrain; stress routes should include uneven rows and seasonal clutter.

Transferable thinking tool

Preserve map capacity for action-relevant semantic structures, not just photometric fit.

Strong for agricultural and structured outdoor robots; less direct for unstructured indoor SLAM.

#7Tier A - abstract-only

An Action Is Worth One Patch: Unified World-Action Modeling with PatchWAM

world-action modeling - action-as-patch interface - shared generative backbone

Status quo belief

World models usually add dedicated action heads or separate action experts to connect visual representations to control.

Friction / anomaly

Separate heads can hide whether the visual generative backbone itself contains the action-relevant dynamics.

Hidden premise

A continuous action can be written into a representation compatible with image patches and predicted by the same backbone.

Conceptual move

Treat continuous actions as another patch type through Action-as-Patch so one model predicts both robot motion and future scene state.

Evidence trace

  • [Abstract]PatchWAM reports gains over a matched dual-expert control in subsampled training windows.
  • [Abstract]Full-data evaluations report 91.8% success on LIBERO-Plus and 96.12% on RoboTwin 2.0 with augmented demonstrations.
  • [Inference]APRL should test whether action-as-patch changes failure recovery and contact behavior, not only benchmark success.

Mechanism

The abstract says visual prediction and action generation become one generative process without a dedicated action head or separate expert.

Falsification frontier

If action-as-patch works only after heavy demonstration augmentation, the inherited capacity claim weakens under low-data deployment.

Adversarial read

Patch representation may obscure continuous control constraints; perturb action patches and evaluate physical feasibility.

Transferable thinking tool

When adding a new robot signal, first ask whether the interface can inherit an existing generative backbone.

Strong for patch-tokenized visual policies; less direct for low-level torque controllers.

#8Tier A - abstract-only

ForeDrive: Foresight-Guided End-to-End Autonomous Driving with a Planning-Relevant Latent World Model

driving world model - planning-relevant latent - multi-horizon guidance

Status quo belief

Latent world models are often optimized for future predictability and used as pretraining or auxiliary supervision.

Friction / anomaly

A predictable future representation may not be useful for trajectory generation, especially when horizon reliability varies.

Hidden premise

Future latents should guide planning without overriding the current observation when predicted futures are unreliable.

Conceptual move

Couple a JEPA-style multi-horizon latent future representation asymmetrically to a Diffusion Transformer planner.

Evidence trace

  • [Abstract]Planning gradients update the shared online encoder while stop-gradient routing trains the latent predictor with forecasting losses only.
  • [Abstract]ForeDrive reports 89.9 PDMS on NAVSIM v1 and 90.0 one-stage EPDMS on NAVSIM v2 using only the current front-view image at inference.
  • [Inference]APRL should evaluate whether predicted future latents change action ranking under uncertainty, not only future reconstruction.

Mechanism

The abstract uses gated visual fusion, future-status injection, and Trajectory-Adaptive Bias to inject future latents as guidance.

Falsification frontier

If gated future latents do not improve closed-loop safety or recovery, the planning relevance may be open-loop benchmark-specific.

Adversarial read

Future-status gates can hide overconfidence; stress with occlusion, interaction-heavy scenes, and rare actor behavior.

Transferable thinking tool

A world model should expose the reliability of each future horizon before the planner consumes it.

Strong for driving/planning; needs translation for manipulation where future visual tokens depend on contact state.

#9Tier A - abstract-only

NavSafe-infinity: Benchmarking Closed-Loop Driving Safety in Photorealistic Environments

closed-loop driving safety - traffic taxonomy - open-loop blind spot

Status quo belief

End-to-end driving policies are commonly advanced on open-loop benchmarks.

Friction / anomaly

Open-loop gains can fail to predict compounding errors, recovery behavior, or safety interaction with other actors.

Hidden premise

Safety should be measured by scenario-level success and failure criteria under closed-loop feedback, not by imitation quality alone.

Conceptual move

Create a photorealistic closed-loop benchmark with 280 scenarios across 28 event types and category-level traffic-safety scores.

Evidence trace

  • [Abstract]Evaluation of 20 E2E policies finds that open-loop gains do not reliably transfer to closed-loop safety.
  • [Abstract]Open-loop RL fine-tuning can reward hack by trading safety margin for ego progress, which closed-loop feedback amplifies.
  • [Inference]APRL should require closed-loop event taxonomies before accepting open-loop driving or navigation policy gains.

Mechanism

The abstract defines failures through a structured taxonomy covering crashes, vulnerable road users, violations, and incidents.

Falsification frontier

If closed-loop event categories do not predict real road or robot-route incidents, the taxonomy needs field calibration.

Adversarial read

Photorealistic simulation can still miss social or sensor failures; include domain randomization and real replay where possible.

Transferable thinking tool

Open-loop improvement is not safety evidence until closed-loop recovery and actor interaction are tested.

Direct for autonomous driving; useful as a template for mobile-robot navigation safety suites.

#10Tier A - abstract-only

RoboFollow: Unveiling the Instruction Following Mirage in Embodied Agents

instruction following - low scene entropy - VLA/WAM diagnosis

Status quo belief

Embodied agents can achieve high success when the scene visually implies the task, making language appear less necessary.

Friction / anomaly

A policy can score highly while barely using the instruction if the scene supports only one valid branch.

Hidden premise

Instruction-following benchmarks need high scene entropy and controlled perturbations that isolate comprehension from motor execution.

Conceptual move

Design a diagnostic benchmark with high scene entropy, a four-level perturbation protocol, and confound-controlled Intent and Execution scores.

Evidence trace

  • [Abstract]Strong L0 performance does not reliably transfer to L1-L3 under the reported fine-tuning setup.
  • [Abstract]Representative mitigations fail to close the instruction-following gap.
  • [Inference]APRL should add high-entropy paired instructions before claiming language-conditioned manipulation or navigation.

Mechanism

The abstract evaluates nine VLA and WAM policies and tests mitigations such as stronger VLM backbones, QA co-training, LangForce, and classifier-free guidance.

Falsification frontier

If models trained with richer high-entropy data close L1-L3 without architectural changes, the failure is data coverage rather than instruction interface.

Adversarial read

The protocol restricts actions to the trained repertoire; open-world commands may reveal additional planning failures.

Transferable thinking tool

Separate whether the robot followed language from whether the scene already made the action obvious.

Strong for VLA/WAM instruction benchmarks; needs task-specific entropy design for field robots.
