Weekly conclusion: W37 made robot intelligence an evidence-commitment problem. Monday asked whether VLA success, maps, world models, VLM explanations, and efficient memories expose the cue that should change action before failure. Wednesday enlarged that into evidence freshness: policies, maps, occupancy, generated physics, driving scenes, and navigation beliefs had to show when their support goes stale. Thursday made the same contract sharper through verifier-aware post-training, uncertainty-aware geometry, relative driving risk, evidence-order VLM calibration, and irreversible token admission. Friday then converted the week into transition accountability: a memory, generated future, 3DGS map, safety layer, cache, or dexterous contact structure is useful only if it states which transition it may authorize and which deployment condition revokes that authority.

September 7, 9, 10, and 11 formed a compressed week because September 8 had no cs.CV or cs.RO arXiv listing. The compression made the research decision clearer rather than weaker: across 1,055 deduplicated pastweek papers and 453 ROI papers, the strongest robotics signal was evidence authority. VLA, geometry, world models, autonomy safety, VLM reliability, efficient agents, dexterity, and navigation all asked whether a cue is fresh, physical, grounded, and useful enough to alter the next action.

The weekly counts are useful context, not the conclusion. Robot Learning led with 108 ROI papers, Generation followed with 87, and 3D/Scene reached 59; the deeper shift is that these buckets now share the same release question. Evidence should enter the robot loop only when it changes transition choice, recovery timing, safety margin, map validity, or privacy and compute risk in a measurable way.

FailureSpot: Label-Efficient Timestamp-Level Failure Detection for Vision-Language-Action Models

Reading depth: Tier A - abstract-only

Status quo belief: VLA failure detection is often evaluated at the trajectory level, after the policy has already taken wrong actions.

Friction: The abstract says trajectory-level labels turn normal pre-failure behavior into noisy failure supervision.

Conceptual move: Use unlabeled action chunks to generate weak timestamp signals, then spend dense labels only on uncertain trajectories.

Mechanism: The method detects inconsistent consecutive chunks, frozen or idle actions, and aggressive random motions before fine-tuning with active timestamp labels.

Decisive evidence: [Abstract]: The paper targets fine-grained timestamp-level VLA failure detection.; [Abstract]: It combines action-derived weak supervision with active learning for timestamp annotation.; [Inference]: APRL should ask how early a detector changes continuation, recovery, or stop decisions before terminal failure.

Falsification frontier: If action-derived weak labels mostly mark benign exploration or pause behavior, timestamp localization can become a style detector rather than a failure detector.

Adversarial read: Evaluate false alarms on slow cautious policies and missed alarms on smooth but wrong object-selection failures.

Transferable thinking tool: Treat failure as a time-localized control variable, not a post-hoc episode attribute.

CR-VLA-Force: Learning Control-aware Compliance VLA Model for Robust Contact-rich Robotic Manipulation

Reading depth: Tier A - abstract-only

Status quo belief: Force-aware VLA policies can still execute long action chunks before force feedback changes the command.

Friction: The abstract says force-aware VLA models remain weak at precise force tracking and rapid successive adjustment.

Conceptual move: Encode force signal sequences with visual-language features and treat compliance as a control-aware VLA capability.

Mechanism: The proposed CC-VLA framework uses multimodal mixture-of-experts features so force and visual-language evidence can jointly shape reactive control.

Decisive evidence: [Abstract]: The paper targets action-chunk latency and force-tracking limits in contact-rich manipulation.; [Abstract]: It introduces Control-aware Compliance VLA for reactive control with force/torque perception.; [Inference]: APRL should score whether force evidence changes the remaining command before a contact failure completes.

Falsification frontier: If gains disappear when contact timing, material compliance, or object geometry changes, the method may be fitting familiar force profiles.

Adversarial read: Test delayed force spikes, near-identical visual states with different contact stiffness, and action chunks that must be interrupted mid-execution.

Transferable thinking tool: Treat contact as a permission signal over the next action, not as an auxiliary observation.

HaWMPO: Hallucination-Aware World Model-based Policy Optimization for Generalist Robot Policy

Reading depth: Tier A - abstract-only

Status quo belief: VLA post-training can use online robot reinforcement learning, but physical rollouts are costly, slow, and risky.

Friction: The abstract says world-model rollouts can hallucinate long-horizon state transitions and mislead policy learning.

Conceptual move: Attach hallucination scores to action-conditioned generated image sequences and soften policy optimization rewards accordingly.

Mechanism: HaWMPO estimates generated-rollout reliability and uses a Reward-Soft mechanism to suppress unreliable action chunks during GRPO-style post-training.

Decisive evidence: [Abstract]: The paper targets world-model hallucinations in long-horizon VLA policy post-training.; [Abstract]: It reports LIBERO gains and real-world G1 manipulation improvement from 67.5 percent to 80.0 percent across two tasks.; [Inference]: APRL should weight imagined robot rollouts by hallucination risk before using them as training evidence.

Falsification frontier: If hallucination scores fail under new objects, cameras, or contact regimes, the optimizer may still amplify plausible but wrong trajectories.

Adversarial read: Compare imagined rollouts with physical rollouts under hidden object displacement, long-horizon dependency, and contact-state changes.

Transferable thinking tool: A world model is a source of proposed evidence, not a free substitute for physical interaction.

View-Structured Conformal Prediction for 3D Gaussian Splatting

Reading depth: Tier A - abstract-only

Status quo belief: 3DGS uncertainty maps are often visually inspected without finite-sample guarantees for a whole rendered view.

Friction: The abstract says marginal pixel coverage can look acceptable while view-level coverage fails.

Conceptual move: Treat novel-view synthesis as structured regression and require coverage over a fraction of pixels in a new view.

Mechanism: VSCP separates renderer-provided spatial shape from a transferable view-difficulty multiplier, then calibrates across held-out views.

Decisive evidence: [Abstract]: Pixel-pooled calibration reaches 89.9 percent marginal pixel coverage but only 61.4 percent view-event coverage at a 90 percent target.; [Abstract]: View-CP reaches 91.7 to 92.0 percent view-event coverage in the reported setting.; [Inference]: APRL should ask whether 3D maps report action-relevant uncertainty at the view or task level.

Falsification frontier: If calibrated view boxes do not predict downstream relocalization or collision-query risk, uncertainty remains visual rather than robotic.

Adversarial read: Evaluate viewpoint sweeps where small uncovered regions contain the obstacle, target handle, or localization feature needed for action.

Transferable thinking tool: Uncertainty must be calibrated at the unit where decisions are made.

HuRo: Robotizing Human Videos for Scalable VLA Pretraining

Reading depth: Tier A - abstract-only

Status quo belief: Large human video corpora look attractive for robot policy learning, but embodiment mismatch can make visual imitation non-executable.

Friction: The abstract says existing approaches either robotize task-matched videos or separately align observation and action at scale.

Conceptual move: Convert heterogeneous human videos into robot-aligned observations and action trajectories, then test whether scale helps VLA pretraining.

Mechanism: HuRo infers missing intermediate signals across annotation levels and builds robotized episodes before downstream manipulation pretraining.

Decisive evidence: [Abstract]: The paper constructs about 630K robotized episodes and 142M processed frames from five human-video sources.; [Abstract]: Increasing robotized pretraining scale improves real-world task completion from 51.5 percent to 80.3 percent and OOD completion from 34.9 percent to 72.2 percent.; [Inference]: APRL should judge human-video pretraining by execution transfer under spatial and visual shifts, not by clip count alone.

Falsification frontier: If gains disappear when action retargeting or visual robotization is stress-tested separately, scale is not the causal explanation.

Adversarial read: Hold video scale fixed and ablate visual robotization, action retargeting, missing-signal inference, and OOD object placement.

Transferable thinking tool: Treat robotized video as executable supervision only after testing which alignment step changes the robot action.

RIDE: Relocalization-Informed Depth Estimation with 3D Gaussian Splatting

Reading depth: Tier A - abstract-only

Status quo belief: Render-match-PnP relocalization is often treated as a pose-recovery tool, while dense depth is learned by a separate model.

Friction: The abstract says relocalization correspondences carry metric geometry that is usually overlooked for depth estimation.

Conceptual move: Fuse PnP-RANSAC inlier depth from a metrically scaled 3DGS model with pretrained video-depth priors for robot RGB streams.

Mechanism: RIDE combines sparse metric depth, global/local correction, and temporal memory to support dense depth through intermittent observations.

Decisive evidence: [Abstract]: The method estimates dense metric depth from a robot RGB stream using a metrically scaled 3DGS model.; [Abstract]: It is evaluated on robot sequences without fine tuning and reports improved depth accuracy and temporal consistency over scale-only calibration.; [Inference]: APRL should evaluate 3DGS maps by depth and relocalization recovery under observation gaps.

Falsification frontier: If improvement vanishes when PnP inliers are sparse or dynamic objects dominate, the map is not robust enough for deployment.

Adversarial read: Sweep occlusion length, map scale error, dynamic clutter, and view angle while measuring depth drift and pose recovery.

Transferable thinking tool: Use relocalization geometry as a correction signal, not just a success/failure pose output.

ReactHuman: A Physics-Grounded Benchmark for Human-Like Reactive Decision-Making in Embodied Multimodal LLMs

Reading depth: Tier A - abstract-only

Status quo belief: Embodied MLLM evaluation often measures passive video understanding or deliberate long-horizon planning.

Friction: The abstract says no prior benchmark measures whether a model can turn physical understanding into immediate safety-critical action.

Conceptual move: Put the evaluated MLLM inside simulated humanoid hazard scenes and execute every committed plan.

Mechanism: ReactHuman uses 17 event families, reproducible rigid-body scenes, adversarial object appearances, and a five-metric reaction suite.

Decisive evidence: [Abstract]: The benchmark contains over 1,000 bit-for-bit reproducible scenes with 240 Hz rigid-body simulation ground truth.; [Abstract]: Seven evaluated MLLMs mishandle roughly one hazard in three, and the failures do not shrink with model scale in the reported study.; [Inference]: APRL should evaluate embodied reasoning by executed reaction safety under misleading appearance and motion cues.

Falsification frontier: If improved controllers solve reaction timing while the language model still chooses unsafe goals, the benchmark conflates policy and motor layers.

Adversarial read: Hold the hazard physics fixed while swapping appearance, reaction deadline, embodiment reach, and available evasive actions.

Transferable thinking tool: Physical understanding earns authority only when the chosen action survives execution.

Caption-once, Frames-on-Demand: Visual-Need Routing for Budget-Aware Agentic Long Video Understanding

Reading depth: Tier A - abstract-only

Status quo belief: Long-video systems often choose between dense visual tokens that are expensive and text summaries that lose fine visual attributes.

Friction: The abstract states that language memories preserve temporal structure better, while pixels remain decisive for attribute-level perception.

Conceptual move: Create a dual-track narrative index and route each query through a Visual-Need Router before bounded keyframe retrieval.

Mechanism: An edge pass builds story and micro-log memory; cloud-side reasoning retrieves frames only for perceptual questions.

Decisive evidence: [Abstract]: The router triggers keyframe retrieval for perceptual questions and keeps temporal-structural questions in language space.; [Abstract]: The framework caps per-query frame consumption regardless of video length while reporting strong accuracy-efficiency trade-offs.; [Inference]: APRL should treat robot video memory as query-conditioned evidence admission, not a fixed frame budget.

Falsification frontier: If decisive safety cues are rare and not predictable from the language memory, the router may skip exactly the frames needed for action.

Adversarial read: Place task-critical visual changes in brief off-center frames and ask queries whose language story sounds sufficient but is wrong.

Transferable thinking tool: Retrieve pixels only when the decision contract names what text cannot know.
