#1Tier A - abstract-only

Validating, Not Sampling: Region-Level Robustness of Vision-Language and Vision-Language-Action Models

VLA robustness validation - continuous perturbation regions - action sensitivity

Status quo belief

VLM and VLA robustness is often estimated by sampling a handful of perturbed inputs.

Friction / anomaly

A sampled pass does not certify that no nearby perturbation changes the robot decision.

Hidden premise

For robot actions, robustness should be a property of an input region, not a point estimate.

Conceptual move

Validate entire continuous photometric and geometric perturbation regions for VLMs and VLAs.

Evidence trace

  • [Abstract]The paper validates brightness shifts, camera rotations, and their composition over continuous regions.
  • [Abstract]It reports that VLAs can change commanded actions under perturbations as small as plus or minus one degree.
  • [Inference]APRL should treat camera pose and exposure perturbation regions as release gates for manipulation policies.

Mechanism

The abstract introduces H2V-M, a margin-aware convergence rule that searches perturbation regions efficiently at large-model scale.

Falsification frontier

If region-level counterexamples do not predict closed-loop failures or recoverability, the certificate is conservative but not decision-relevant.

Adversarial read

Separate harmless action variation from contact-critical action variation; otherwise the validator can overstate risk.

Transferable thinking tool

Replace random perturbation sampling with region-level action invariance tests.

Direct for vision-conditioned actions; less direct for policies with explicit geometric state estimation and downstream safety filters.

#2Tier A - abstract-only

LIBERO-VPro: Benchmarking Closed-Loop Visual Robustness of Robotic Foundation Models

closed-loop visual robustness - robotic foundation models - perturbation taxonomy

Status quo belief

Robotic foundation models are usually tested under clean, timely, and consistent observations.

Friction / anomaly

Nominal success can hide brittle grounding when visual evidence is degraded, stale, inconsistent, or task-relevantly changed.

Hidden premise

Visual robustness is multi-dimensional and architecture-dependent, so one clean benchmark score cannot certify deployment.

Conceptual move

Perturb visual evidence during closed-loop execution across degradation, staleness, source consistency, and task-relevant scene variation.

Evidence trace

  • [Abstract]The benchmark perturbs visual evidence throughout execution rather than only at the start.
  • [Abstract]The results show different robustness profiles for VLAs and WAMs.
  • [Inference]APRL should keep stale camera, local cue disruption, and changed precondition tests separate.

Mechanism

The abstract describes 12 challenge categories, 96 settings, and 3,296 task-condition cases evaluated across simulated and real rollouts.

Falsification frontier

If perturbation families do not change model rankings or recovery choices, the benchmark may be too broad to guide design.

Adversarial read

Real-world perturbations can co-occur; testing one axis at a time may miss coupled failures.

Transferable thinking tool

Treat visual robustness as a set of closed-loop failure families, not one corruption score.

Strong for manipulation foundation models; needs adaptation for field robots with non-visual redundancy.

compressed VLA policy - offline validation failure - closed-loop causal forensics

Status quo belief

Compression is often accepted when offline imitation metrics or teacher ratios remain high.

Friction / anomaly

A compressed policy can pass every offline check and still collapse to zero closed-loop success.

Hidden premise

Closed-loop deployment distribution and action-trace signatures can reveal failure modes that marginal offline statistics miss.

Conceptual move

Dissect one naturally occurring collapse with paired action traces, intervention tests, and deployment-distribution teacher rollouts.

Evidence trace

  • [Abstract]The compressed Octo-Base student passes offline checks but scores 0/72 in the simulated closed-loop task.
  • [Abstract]A minimal-pair intervention using deployment-distribution teacher rollouts restores teacher-like behavior.
  • [Inference]APRL should require a few dozen closed-loop trials before accepting compressed VLA policies.

Mechanism

The abstract isolates a late-heavy negative z residual and shows that deployment-distribution rollouts remove it and restore parity.

Falsification frontier

If the collapse signature is specific to one architecture and task, it is a case study rather than a general compression law.

Adversarial read

Check whether the repaired policy overfits the deployment rollouts and fails under object or camera shifts.

Transferable thinking tool

Audit compression by closed-loop causal signatures, not teacher-ratio averages.

Strong for compressed policies; less direct for safety-filtered modular controllers.

#4Tier A - abstract-only

DexTacWAM: A Visuo-Tactile World-Action Model for Dexterous Manipulation

visuo-tactile WAM - dexterous contact dynamics - tactile latent prediction

Status quo belief

World-action models usually inherit video priors but remain vision-centric at deployment.

Friction / anomaly

Dexterous contact dynamics are only partly visible, so vision-only WAMs can miss slip, force, or finger-specific contact state.

Hidden premise

A tactile latent should enter the predicted world state, not merely condition an action head.

Conceptual move

Encode each fingertip, compress tactile features with finger and pose awareness, and inject touch into video diffusion world modeling.

Evidence trace

  • [Abstract]DexTacWAM models distributed multi-finger contact dynamics on a 22-DoF bimanual platform.
  • [Abstract]Removing tactile world modeling substantially lowers the reported four-task mean.
  • [Inference]APRL should distinguish tactile feature conditioning from tactile future-state modeling.

Mechanism

The abstract attributes gains to modeling contact evolution as part of the predicted world state rather than tactile conditioning alone.

Falsification frontier

If touch improves the benchmark but does not change recovery timing or contact failure avoidance, it is predictive but not authoritative.

Adversarial read

Evaluate unseen materials, changing fingertip calibration, and object geometries where tactile priors can overfit.

Transferable thinking tool

Make contact evolution a predicted state variable that can change action.

Direct for tactile dexterous manipulation; weaker for grippers without reliable tactile sensing.

#5Tier A - abstract-only

HapticWAM: Distilling Imagined Touch into a World-Action Model without Inference-Time Tactile Sensing

tactile distillation - contact package prediction - deployment without fingertip input

Status quo belief

Tactile sensors help contact tasks, but deployment may not support fingertip observations at inference time.

Friction / anomaly

The policy still needs force, slip, and contact geometry estimates when direct tactile observations are unavailable.

Hidden premise

A student can retain a generative contact head that imagines future contact mechanics from vision and action history.

Conceptual move

Distill heterogeneous tactile contact futures and action predictions into a WAM that removes fingertip input branches at deployment.

Evidence trace

  • [Abstract]The model predicts a contact package, not only tactile pixels.
  • [Abstract]The student removes fingertip input branches while retaining the generative contact head.
  • [Inference]APRL can test whether imagined touch is enough to delay, refine, or veto action chunks.

Mechanism

The abstract describes a teacher with gel images, deformation, shear, forces, wrench, and derived contact state, then a student with imagined touch.

Falsification frontier

If imagined touch fails exactly when contact becomes novel, the distilled representation cannot replace deployment tactile sensing.

Adversarial read

Stress transparent, deformable, and high-friction objects where visual contact cues mislead the contact head.

Transferable thinking tool

Use tactile supervision to train a contact-state simulator that survives sensor removal.

Useful for low-cost deployment; risky for high-force assembly where missing touch can cause damage.

#6Tier A - abstract-only

Splat-CBF: Safe Next-Best-View Control in 3D Gaussian-Splat Maps

3D Gaussian maps - safe active perception - control barrier functions

Status quo belief

Active perception often treats next-best-view and collision avoidance as separate planning objectives.

Friction / anomaly

The most informative regions are often the least trusted for collision margins, so information gain can pull the robot toward unsafe space.

Hidden premise

Perception should be a soft objective only after safety is encoded as a hard motion constraint.

Conceptual move

Use a risk-aware CBF over Gaussian-field uncertainty for safety and a second perception barrier for expected Fisher information.

Evidence trace

  • [Abstract]The method enforces collision avoidance as a hard constraint while steering toward informative views.
  • [Abstract]It reports indoor simulations and robot experiments on manipulator and Ackermann platforms.
  • [Inference]APRL should evaluate map representations by safe information gain along the planned path.

Mechanism

The abstract describes a quadratic program where safety is hard, perception is soft, and slack adapts near uncertain regions.

Falsification frontier

If the slack schedule frequently sacrifices perception near the very regions that matter, next-best-view gains may not reach downstream tasks.

Adversarial read

Test dynamic obstacles and reflective clutter where Gaussian uncertainty can understate collision risk.

Transferable thinking tool

Treat view selection as a constrained control problem, not a pure reconstruction objective.

Strong for exploration and inspection; less direct for static offline reconstruction.

#9Tier A - abstract-only

Robot World Models Are Not Invariant to How the Actions Are Written

action parameterization - world model invariance - latent rollout disagreement

Status quo belief

Action parameterization is often treated as an implementation detail if encodings are mutually reconstructible.

Friction / anomaly

A world model can answer a different future-prediction question when the same trajectory is written in another action coordinate system.

Hidden premise

World-model invariance must be audited in the action channel, not only in visual input transformations.

Conceptual move

Test whether latent dynamics are invariant to absolute versus delta action encodings and repair the defect through objective averaging and disagreement penalties.

Evidence trace

  • [Abstract]The two encodings are mutually reconstructible, yet the world model's beliefs become near-orthogonal in a PushT case.
  • [Abstract]The repair depends on where averaging is applied; output averaging is not generally available for direction-valued prediction.
  • [Inference]APRL should treat action representation as a model contract that needs invariance tests.

Mechanism

The abstract reports large retrieval and action-selection degradation when trained and evaluated under mismatched action parameterizations.

Falsification frontier

If controller performance is insensitive to the latent disagreement, the defect may matter more for model evaluation than deployment.

Adversarial read

Test mixed action spaces, normalized joints, end-effector deltas, and contact-conditioned action chunks.

Transferable thinking tool

Audit the action channel as a source of hidden world-model failure.

Direct for WAMs and imitation policies; less direct for planners with explicit dynamics models.

Frontier memory

Strengthening

September 21 emphasized early evidence timing for VLA failure, contact, mapping, social navigation, and recovery authority.

September 22 strengthens the same axis, but shifts from first warning time to validation contracts: perturbation regions, stale evidence, tactile authority, uncertainty-aware maps, and action-encoding invariance.

New signal

Recent reports treated tactile WAMs and safety filters as separate lines.

Today they converge: touch, force, barrier rollouts, conformal uncertainty, and recovery data all act as gates that decide whether a nominal command keeps authority.

Commoditizing

VLA, WAM, 3DGS, and Gaussian SLAM now recur almost every run.

The differentiator is no longer proposing one of these backbones; it is proving which interface variable survives closed-loop stress and changes the robot action.

Contradiction

Leaderboard and nominal benchmark progress often imply that better models transfer directly.

Counterfactual driving, closed-loop VLA collapse, LIBERO-VPro, and region validation argue that nominal success can be almost orthogonal to deployment behavior.

Missing axis

The repo has many per-modality diagnostics but few cross-modality arbitration rules.

A shared arbitration benchmark is still missing: when camera, touch, map uncertainty, action encoding, and safety filter disagree, which signal should own the next command?
